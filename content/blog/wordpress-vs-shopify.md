The honest version: if you sell products, Shopify. If you publish content and take enquiries, WordPress (or something lighter). Everything else is detail.

## Side by side

| | WordPress | Shopify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best at | Content, brochure sites, blogs | Selling physical products |
| Payments/tax/inventory | Plugins you maintain | Built in |
| Hosting & security | Yours to manage | Handled |
| Monthly cost | Hosting + plugin licences | Plan + apps |
| Design freedom | Total | High, within a theme system |
| Maintenance burden | Real and ongoing | Low |

## Where WordPress still wins

- Content-heavy sites with hundreds of articles
- Complex editorial workflows and multiple authors
- Anything needing deep custom post types
- You already have a team who knows it

## Where Shopify wins outright

- Real inventory, variants, shipping rules and tax
- Checkout you don't have to secure yourself
- Multi-currency and multi-region selling
- You'd rather sell than patch plugins

## The WooCommerce trap

WooCommerce is fine when it's already working. Choosing it fresh means you now own hosting, security, PCI scope, plugin conflicts and performance — for a checkout Shopify gives you for a monthly fee.

## The third option nobody mentions

If you're a service business with under fifteen pages, you may need neither. A static, code-built site loads faster than either, has no plugin surface to attack, and costs nothing monthly beyond hosting.
