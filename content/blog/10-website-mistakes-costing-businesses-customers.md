Ten leaks I find on almost every site I audit, in rough order of how much money they cost.

## 1. It's slow on mobile

Anything over three seconds and a third of visitors leave before seeing anything. Test it on 4G, not your office wifi — or run a free [speed audit](/insights).

## 2. The phone number isn't tappable

If it's an image or plain text, mobile users have to memorise it. They won't.

## 3. One "Services" page for everything

Google can only rank a page for what it's about. Five services on one page means you rank for none of them properly.

## 4. Stock photos of people who don't work there

Every visitor spots it instantly. One real photo beats ten polished fakes.

## 5. No pricing signal at all

You don't have to publish a price list. You do have to say "projects typically start around X" so people can self-qualify instead of bouncing.

## 6. A contact form with eleven fields

Name, contact, and what they need. Everything else you can ask on the call.

## 7. No proof

Reviews, logos, before-and-afters, case numbers. Testimonials belong on the page, not just on Google.

## 8. Broken or orphaned pages

Pages nothing links to, 404s from an old redesign, dead menu items. Fix the redirect map — see [Website Redesign](/solutions/website-redesign).

## 9. No local signals

No address, no service areas, no LocalBusiness schema. That's most of [Local SEO](/solutions/local-seo) missing.

## 10. Nothing is measured

No GA4, no Search Console, no conversion events. If you can't see which page produces enquiries, every decision after this is a guess.

## Fixing them

Most of these take a day, not a rebuild. Start with speed, tappable contact, and one page per service — those three usually move the number on their own.
