This comes up in almost every first call: "I've got a Google listing, do I even need a website?"

Short answer: yes — but not for the reason most people assume.

## What each one is good at

| | Google Business Profile | Website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free | One-off build cost |
| Shows in Maps | Yes | No |
| You control it | Partly | Completely |
| Ranks for service keywords | Weakly | Yes |
| Can be suspended overnight | Yes | No |
| Sells your story | Barely | Yes |

## They are not alternatives — one feeds the other

Google ranks your profile partly on signals it reads from your website: consistent name/address/phone, a matching category, local schema, and pages that actually describe the services you claim in your profile. **A good website makes your profile rank better.** A profile with no site behind it caps out fast.

## Where each one wins

- **Profile wins** the "near me, right now" search. Someone needs a locksmith in fifteen minutes and never leaves Maps.
- **Website wins** the considered purchase. Kitchen renovation, legal advice, a $40k build — nobody chooses that from a map pin. They read.

## The suspension problem

Profiles get suspended for edits, category changes, or a competitor's malicious report. If your entire lead flow lives there, a suspension is an outage with no support line. Your website is the one asset nobody can switch off.

## What to do this week

1. Complete every field on your profile — all of them
2. Add ten real photos with real filenames
3. Make sure your website's contact details match **exactly**
4. Add LocalBusiness schema to your site
5. Set up a steady, honest review drip
