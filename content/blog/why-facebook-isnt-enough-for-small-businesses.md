Facebook is a great place to prove you exist. It's a terrible place to be the only place you exist.

## The four things a page can't do

1. **Rank for what people search.** Nobody types "plumber near me" into Facebook. Search happens on Google, and Google ranks pages, not posts.
2. **Convert on your terms.** No custom quote flow, no booking, no upsell path, no analytics worth the name.
3. **Survive a policy change.** Organic reach for local pages has fallen for a decade and will keep falling. You do not control that dial.
4. **Belong to you.** Account locked, page hacked, wrong appeal outcome — and your entire customer list is gone.

## Rented land vs owned land

Social platforms are rented land. You build a shopfront, they own the street, and they can change the rent, the footpath or the opening hours whenever they want. A website is the only piece of your online presence you own outright.

## The right split

- **Facebook / Instagram**: proof, personality, recent work, community
- **Website**: search visibility, credibility, conversion, email capture
- **Google Business Profile**: the map pack and "open now" searches

Every social post should point somewhere you own. That's the whole strategy.
